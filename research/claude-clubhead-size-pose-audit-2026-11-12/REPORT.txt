1. THE DIAGNOSIS
=====================================================================================================================
The model is not mainly the wrong size and it is not un-tilted. It is in the wrong PLACE, and it is drawn WHOLE where
only part of the head can be seen. Three things, in order of how much of the picture they explain:

 A. Near impact the fitted outline is not on the head at all (shots 3 and 4).                               [R]
    On the last frame before contact a diffuse region BRIGHTER than the empty scene stands above the sharp head,
    and the resting ball is still visible through it. The application's toe and top-line samples sit on the edge
    of that region; its "sole" sits on a brightness boundary inside the head. Result at this pin:
        shot 3 f43: model top 7 px = 16 mm above the real head's top edge; 49 % of the drawn model is off the head
        shot 4 f44: model top 14 px = 29 mm above it;                      67 % of the drawn model is off the head
    That region is not the head: its top is 23 to 26 mm higher, relative to the ferrule, than the head's top was
    one frame (3.6 ms) earlier, while the shaft's direction changed by less than 1 degree. A rigid club cannot do
    that. I read it as turf or infill thrown up ahead of the head once it reaches the mat; I have not established
    what it is, only what it is not.

 B. Near impact about 15 mm of the real head is out of sight (shots 3 and 4).                               [R]
    Between the last two frames before contact the ferrule, hosel and top edge all drop about 19 mm, and the lowest
    visible row of the head does not move. Ferrule to lowest visible row: 63.6 -> 45.7 mm (shot 3), 64.5 -> 47.2 mm
    (shot 4). The head has gone below the line of sight over the turf: its sole ends roughly 15 to 25 mm under the
    row the resting ball's bottom sits on. So the visible head is a sliver about 19 px tall where the model draws
    a complete 24 to 25 px face, 7 to 14 px too high. That is the "far too tall, wrong shape, not tilted" picture.
    The owner's remark that the bottom is hidden by the mat is correct and is the larger part of the size complaint.

 C. Shot 1 is a different, smaller error.                                                                   [R]
    No bright region and nothing hidden on f43. Toe and bottom agree with the model; the model's top is 4 px = 7 mm
    above the real top edge, and its shaft axis is 8 px = 14 mm heel-ward of the real shaft. On both shot-1 frames
    the head looks about 10 % smaller than the model at the supplied depth and angles (best single size 0.90).
    I cannot separate "this swing's head was more open and more lofted than supplied" from "depth" on one view.

What it is NOT:
  - Not un-tilted. The supplied D-plane loft, face and roll are applied; my independent projection matches the
    application's own outline pixels to 0.0001 px. But tilting a FLAT face outline by the loft only squeezes its
    height by cos(loft): the whole outline moves 1 to 3 px. Setting all three angles to zero moves it 1.1 to 2.9 px.
    The owner's eye is right that the tilt makes no visible difference; the cause is that a flat outline seen from
    behind barely changes with tilt, not that the angles are ignored.                                       [R]
  - Not a drawing-to-mm or aspect error. One mm scale serves both axes from the kiosk drawing to the projector.
    With the whole head visible in clear air the generic outline covers this club's own silhouette at IoU 0.83,
    0.90, 0.90 by translation alone; the best single size is 0.90, 0.95, 1.05 (same club), mean 0.97.       [R]
  - Not camera scale. The resting ball's measured diameter fixes mm per px at the ball to about 5 %; the head is
    53 to 101 mm nearer on the last frame (3 to 6 %).                                                       [R]
  - Not the planar representation as such. Placed correctly, face outline plus sole strip leaves 4 to 16 % of the
    silhouette area unexplained. What the representation lacks is a ground line, a 3-D hosel, and any notion that
    edges inside the head are not its outline.

Heel-toe remains roughly right throughout (toe tip is visible and sharp). Height is what is wrong, and for shots 3
and 4 it was wrong by the whole of A plus B.

7. THE REPAIR, IN ORDER (no production change was made in this audit)
=====================================================================================================================
There is no small patch. The correction is to what the fit observes, so I did not write a production test and a
one-line fix that would misrepresent it. Write these failing tests FIRST, then change the fitter:
  T1 recorded fixtures (development): last frame of shots 3 and 4. The fitted model's top must lie within 4 px of
     the cavity's upper edge net of the rim. Fails at 0fa9870b by 7 and 14 px.
  T2 generated: a head whose lower 15 mm is behind an opaque ground line. The placement must not move up when the
     ground rises. Needs a renderer with a ground; it has none today.
  T3 generated: a semi-transparent region 20 to 40 DN brighter than the scene above the head, ball visible through
     it. The placement must not move.
  T4 rigid consistency: across consecutive frames the model's top-to-ferrule distance may not change by more than a
     bound (say 6 mm) unless the shaft direction changes. At 0fa9870b the fit has no ferrule and no such check.
  T5 overlay: nothing is drawn solid below the lowest visible row.
Then:
  R1 [P] Anchor the head to the shaft. Shaft axis and ferrule are sharp on every frame, above turf and thrown
     material, before and after contact. Place the model on the last clear-air frame or two (whole head visible),
     carry it rigidly to the bracket frames, interpolate ACROSS contact with the ferrule (observed on both sides)
     instead of dead-reckoning a toe that is never seen.
  R2 [P] Stop fitting the sole. Measure the lowest visible row and treat everything below it as unobserved. Use the
     top edge (cavity upper edge / rim) as the vertical check, searched around the shaft-carried prediction.
  R3 [P] Accept an edge only where the head is DARKER than what it covers or where it agrees with the rigid carry.
     Brighter, diffuse evidence above the head is rejected.
  R4 [V][R] Put the hosel foot in 3-D (4.5 to 6.2 mm today). Then let the shaft carry weight; today it gets none on
     shot 1 (0 of 8 rows kept) and 2 to 3 rows on shots 3 and 4.
  R5 [P] Carry the rotation: w per frame (1.06 to 1.10 here) for the turn, the toe peak's drift against the ferrule
     for the loft change. This is what the 2-D carry lacks (3 px on shot 1).
  R6 [P] Draw overlays clipped at the lowest visible row, hidden part dashed. The owner then sees the same sliver the
     camera sees, with the rest of the face shown as inferred.
  R7 Adopt or reject the existing lens calibration's principal point on its own evidence. Minor for this problem.
Validation: an improved overlay is not strike accuracy. Accuracy needs new marked shots the method has never seen,
with the mark read against a scale. The four October shots cannot provide it.

8. CORRECTIONS TO MY PREVIOUS AUDIT (research/claude-clubhead-pose-ml-audit-2026-11-12)
=====================================================================================================================
  1. REPORT section 1 ranked "two free numbers per frame" first and "the sole is not one edge" second. For shots 3
     and 4 the dominant cause is A above, which I did not find. I had the evidence in hand and misread it:
     "42 and 45 % of the ball pixels the model covers still show the ball" is the model sitting on the bright
     region. I reported it as occupancy and moved on.
  2. EVIDENCE.txt "visible minus model, top +0.2 .. +2.2 px" for shots 3 and 4 measured the bright region's top, not
     the head's. Wrong. The head's top is 7 and 14 px below the model's.
  3. "Fitted sole 11.6 and 14.7 mm above the hitting surface" assumed the head stays above the surface. It does not:
     the head goes roughly 15 to 25 mm below the ball's bottom row. The direction of that finding stands; its datum
     was wrong.
  4. HANDOFF item 3 proposed reporting "the lowest visible point of the rear body with a visibility flag", and item 5
     tracked "toe tip, shaft line, lowest point". Drop the lowest point as a datum entirely; add the ferrule.
  5. "What the bright bottom band is on shots 3 and 4" (open item): it moves with the ferrule, so it is the club. It
     no longer matters: nothing at the bottom is a usable datum near impact.
  6. Section 1c, "the head turns between frames", stands (w = 1.06 to 1.10) and item [D] on the hosel foot is now
     measured (3.8 and 4.2 mm against a deduced 4.5 and 6.2).
  7. My hitting-surface rows came from an "implied pitch" of 2.1 deg. The inactive calibration's principal point
