How to read it

  • Camera is the rig's view. The solid club should cover the head in the picture; lower the Club solid slider to see the pixels through it.
  • Toe side shows loft: how far the face leans back from vertical.
  • Above shows face angle: how far the blue arrow swings off the line away from the camera.
  • Face-on shows the striking face with the three impact points on it.

What is measured and what is not

  • Where the club sits across the view comes from the pixels: the shaft line, the head's top edge and your trace.
  • The club's distance from the camera is not measured. It is set so the face touches the ball at contact, and moved by club speed times time for the other two frames. The size (0.93) follows from that distance.
  • Face angle 1.4°, loft 26.7° and toe-down 3.5° are supplied by the launch model. The rear view cannot yet check them to better than about 10°.
  • The body's thickness and the score lines are a generic iron drawn for orientation. Only the face outline, sole and shaft axis are used in the fit.
  • The ball after impact is measured: its direction from the camera comes from its edge on frames 44 to 46 (fit within 0.4 px), its distance from that flight and the radar speed. The short orange line through it is the 6 mm either way I would allow in distance.
  • The mat is drawn level through the ball's bottom; the camera is assumed level, so treat it as approximate.
