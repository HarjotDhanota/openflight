## 1. How do TrackMan, FlightScope/Mevo and academic work measure spin, launch angles and club data with Doppler/FMCW radar, and what SNR, dwell, bandwidth and aperture do they need?

### Takeaway
The two commercial radar vendors use CW Doppler plus a multi-receiver phase-comparison ("phased array") receiver. Angles come from inter-receiver phase. Spin comes from periodic phase and amplitude modulation of the ball's Doppler return: either equally spaced sidebands at spin-rate harmonics (TrackMan) or seam-driven modulation at twice the spin rate, boosted by the ball's dielectric-lens effect (FlightScope/EDH). TrackMan does not measure spin axis from micro-Doppler at all. It infers the axis from the lift acceleration of the tracked 3-D trajectory. Neither vendor publishes SNR, dwell or aperture numbers, so those requirements can only be estimated from physics (see Inferences).

### Cited Findings
- TrackMan's spin patent: a point on a spinning ball reflects from positions other than closest-to-radar, so the received signal has "equally spaced sidebands symmetrical around the Doppler shift … with multiple harmonics spaced exactly at the spin frequency". Spin is computed by qualifying equally spaced harmonic tracks and "dividing a frequency of said one qualified harmonic by the respective corresponding harmonic number" — [US8845442B2, Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8845442B2/en)
- The seam modulates the Doppler signal at double the spin rate for most orientations. The exception is a seam exactly perpendicular to the spin axis, where the modulation is minimal. — [TrackMan patent summary via search of US8845442](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8845442B2/en)
- TrackMan spin axis: taken from the trajectory, not the Doppler. Gravity and drag are known, lift is the residual acceleration, and lift is perpendicular to the spin axis ("L•ωe = 0"), solved over multiple trajectory points. The patent text gives no radar frequency, sample rate, window length or antenna count. — [US8845442B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8845442B2/en)
- FlightScope/EDH spin patent (assignee EDH US LLC; inventors Henri Johnson, Thomas Johnson, R. W. Rust): a CW source of "several milliwatts … around 10.525 GHz" with "3 or more receivers" of about 20×20° coverage. A phase-lock tracking filter extracts the time-varying modulation, then an FFT finds the spin frequency. A measured ball with ~3,300 rpm showed its principal oscillation at ~110 Hz (6,600 cycles/min = 2× spin, the seam "double rate modulation"). — [US20140191896A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20140191896)
- The same patent: the ball acts as a dielectric lens (refractive index ~1.8) that magnifies far-surface features. The magnification is "only active for a part of the ball's rotation", about "10-25% of the overall spin period". Both phase and amplitude modulation occur. — [US20140191896A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20140191896)
- Related patent abstracts describe a second cue: Doppler spreading around the mean ball speed that is proportional to spin rate. — [Ball spin rate measurement, US 9868044 (search abstract)](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9868044)
- Mevo+ is described as "phased array Doppler" with "four independent receivers". FlightScope's own technology page says only that a "phased array antenna detects reflected signal and compares phase detected". It gives no band, element count or accuracy. — [FlightScope technology page](https://flightscope.com/pages/technology); [PlayBetter Mevo+ FAQ (reseller)](https://help.playbetter.com/en-US/flightscope-mevo+-faqs-&-specs-35650)
- TrackMan 4 is described as dual radar: a short-range, high-resolution radar on the impact zone for club delivery and a long-range radar for flight. Its radars reportedly sample at "40,000 samples per second and per receiver" to pin down the time of impact. This came from search-result summaries of TrackMan marketing pages and was not verified by a direct fetch. — [TrackMan guide](https://www.trackman.com/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-understanding-trackman); [TrackMan 4 page](https://www.trackman.com/golf/launch-monitors/trackman-4)
- TrackMan's founding radar engineer came from Doppler tracking of bullets and missiles. The original phased-array Doppler patent filing dates to December 2007. — [TrackMan, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TrackMan)
- Academic validity work exists for commercial radar and stereo launch monitors (ICCs "acceptable" for ball speed and spin). I did not read the full paper. — [Leach et al., "How valid and accurate are measurements of golf impact parameters…" (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/319147118_How_valid_and_accurate_are_measurements_of_golf_impact_parameters_obtained_using_commercially_available_radar_and_stereoscopic_optical_launch_monitors)
- Micro-Doppler of spinning projectiles on CW radar has an established modulation model for estimating spin speed. (Artillery context; only the title and abstract were read.) — [A Micro-Doppler Modulation of Spin Projectile on CW Radar](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317119893_A_Micro-Doppler_Modulation_of_Spin_Projectile_on_CW_Radar)

### Inferences
- **The commercial architecture matches neither OpenFlight radar.** Both vendors use one coherent CW transmitter with ≥3–4 simultaneously sampled receivers, so there is no TDM. OpenFlight splits the job: a single-channel CW sensor (OPS243-A), which can give speed but no angle, and a TDM-MIMO FMCW sensor (IWR6843), which gives angle but suffers motion-induced phase error. This supports the memory note "coherent CW + multi-RX receiver". The cheapest route to that architecture on existing hardware may be the IWR6843's continuous-wave test mode (see Q5; unverified).
- **Spin signal model to implement.** Treat the modulation as a periodic pulse train. Lens-focused features are active only 10–25% of a revolution, so the energy spreads across many harmonics. That favours comb or harmonic-sum detection (harmonic product spectrum, cepstrum, or a comb-matched filter over candidate spin rates) over a single-peak search on an envelope spectrum. Also demodulate phase, not just amplitude: both patents say phase modulation is present. Since the current OPS spin path is an amplitude envelope via multitaper, adding the phase residual should raise SNR. This is theory; the size of the gain is untested.
- **Seam 2× ambiguity.** A detected fundamental can be 1× or 2× the spin rate. Resolve it with a physical prior (club loft and speed give an expected spin band) or with the D-plane spin estimate from the camera.
- **Dwell requirement (physics estimate).** Rayleigh frequency resolution is 1/T. The OPS buffer is 4096 samples at 30 kS/s ≈ 136 ms total, including pre-trigger. With ~100 ms of ball-only signal, resolution is ~10 Hz ≈ 600 rpm (at 1× modulation) before interpolation. That is enough for a 1st-order estimate with SNR-limited interpolation. Separating a 1× from a 2× comb needs several cycles, i.e. ≥60 ms at 3,000 rpm. The OPS buffer, not the fps, is the dwell wall, consistent with the memory note "OPS dwell buffer-limited".
- **Doppler-spread cue.** Surface speed is ωr. At 3,000 rpm, ω ≈ 314 rad/s and r = 21.35 mm, giving ~6.7 m/s. At 24.125 GHz that is ±~1.08 kHz of spread around the ball line when the spin axis is perpendicular to the line of sight (backspin seen from behind). The spread edges come from the weakest-RCS parts of the ball, so this cue needs high SNR. At SNR ~1.7 it is unlikely to beat the sideband method.

### Gaps
- No vendor or academic source found giving the SNR, dwell time, receiver count or baseline actually needed for a given spin accuracy. TrackMan and FlightScope keep these proprietary.
- Could not read the ball-marking radar spin patent (US 11673029); the PDF was an image scan. Its assignee and whether the marking is conductive are unconfirmed.
- The TrackMan "40,000 samples/s per receiver" figure is unverified (search summary only).
- How TrackMan and FlightScope measure club path and face with radar was not found in primary text. Only marketing ("dual radar", "fusion tracking") was found.

## 2. IWR6843-class TDM-MIMO best practices: array calibration, TDM motion phase compensation, Doppler ambiguity, angle estimation for fast small targets, multipath/ground bounce

### Takeaway
The biggest error sources for a 70 m/s ball on a 60 GHz TDM array are the velocity-dependent inter-TX phase and Doppler ambiguity, not the choice of angle estimator. The literature fix is to estimate the fractional Doppler phase precisely from same-TX chirps and resolve the integer ambiguity with a hypothesis test, using overlapping virtual elements or a coarse speed. Array calibration should be done in the enclosure at operating range, per angle, not only at boresight. The floor two-ray problem at a 51 mm radar height is largely unresolvable in range at 4 GHz bandwidth. It needs a model-based (symmetric ML / refined ML) estimator or physical mitigation.

### Cited Findings
- **TDM phase error.** With M transmitters, the phase at TX m picks up an extra term 2π·f_D·T_r·(m−1)/M on top of k·d·sinθ. Motion-induced phase becomes indistinguishable from direction-of-arrival phase (angle-Doppler coupling). — [Bechter, Roos, Waldschmidt 2017, "Compensation of Motion-Induced Phase Errors in TDM MIMO Radars" (author PDF)](https://d-nb.info/1161008624/34); [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8052088/)
- **Demonstrated size of the error.** In Bechter's noise-free simulation, a target at 15° moving at 18 m/s was estimated at 18.5° with conventional DFT processing, a 3.5° error. With the adapted Doppler DFT it matched the static reference (15.2°). In their car-mounted measurement at ~18 m/s, the overlapping virtual element showed a 1.54 rad phase discontinuity uncorrected, and the adapted processing removed it. Setup: 2 TX, 10 RX, 594 MHz bandwidth. — [Bechter et al. 2017](https://d-nb.info/1161008624/34)
- **Other compensation options.** Overlapping virtual elements to estimate and correct the error cost (M−1) unique virtual elements. Alternatives are switching-scheme optimisation, estimation/interpolation of the error, and interlaced ramps. — [Bechter et al. 2017](https://d-nb.info/1161008624/34). Model-based joint compensation plus Doppler-unambiguity enhancement is also published. — [Häfner et al., IEEE 2020](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9184222)
- **Ambiguity resolution.** TDM divides the unambiguous velocity by N_tx. Phase-difference-based disambiguation can "regain the maximum unambiguous Doppler which is equivalent to only one transmitter" and "works well when the number of transmitters is large" (abstract only). — [arXiv 2208.03231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.03231). A related TDM/BPM paper: [Phase-based Doppler Disambiguation in TDM and BPM MIMO FMCW Radars](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/349568348_Phase-based_Doppler_Disambiguation_in_TDM_and_BPM_MIMO_FMCW_Radars)
- **TI formulas.** v_max = λ/(4·T_c), where T_c is the full chirp period including idle time. For TDM MIMO it becomes λ/(4·N·T_c). "The actual measurable max velocity can be extended beyond the unambiguous max velocity using higher level algorithm." Range resolution = c/2B, with 4 GHz sweep supported. Slope up to 100 MHz/µs. Idle time "as low as 2 µsec". Complex-1x IF bandwidth = 0.9 × ADC rate. Chirp RAM holds up to 512 chirps, 4 profiles, and per-chirp dithers of start frequency, slope, idle and ADC start. — [TI SWRA553A, Programming Chirp Parameters in TI Radar Devices](https://www.ti.com/lit/pdf/swra553)
- **TI array calibration.** The mmWave SDK `measureRangeBiasAndRxChanPhase 1 X D` uses a strong reflector at distance X and outputs a range bias plus complex gain/phase per virtual antenna (8 pairs on IWR1642). These are applied with `compRangeBiasAndRxChanPhase`. Forum threads report corner reflectors placed well into the far field (>5 m quoted) facing boresight. — [TI E2E: IWR1642 range bias and RX phase with raw ADC](https://e2e.ti.com/support/sensors-group/sensors/f/sensors-forum/1098114/iwr1642boost-implementation-of-range-bias-and-rx-channel-gain-phase-measurement-and-compensation-with-raw-adc-data); [TI E2E: IWR6843AOPEVM calibration](https://e2e.ti.com/support/sensors-group/sensors/f/sensors-forum/1095432/iwr6843aopevm-how-to-do-the-range-bias-and-rx-channel-gain-phase-measurement-and-compensation); [TI E2E: are results valid?](https://e2e.ti.com/support/sensors-group/sensors/f/sensors-forum/1225975/mmwave-sdk-range-bias-and-rx-phase-calibration---are-results-valid)
- **In-situ calibration.** Automatic phase calibration of mmWave arrays from ambient "radio anchors" has been proposed (title and abstract level only). — [arXiv 2506.23472](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.23472)
- **Low-angle multipath.** The classic answer is a maximum-likelihood receiver that exploits the symmetry of direct and specular paths about the array normal. — [IEE Proc. F 1982, "ML receiver for low-angle tracking radar, Part 1: the symmetric case"](https://digital-library.theiet.org/doi/10.1049/ip-f-1.1982.0039). Refined ML (RML) adds geometry plus physical parameters: reflection coefficient, divergence factor and refractivity. — [Frontiers IT&EE 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1631/FITEE.2100003). Wideband range super-resolution can separate direct and reflected paths for low-elevation estimation. — [Sensors 2020, 20(11):3104](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7308873/). Also [MIMO-OFDM multipath elevation, Remote Sensing 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/16/23/4490).

### Inferences
- **Scale of the problem on this rig (my arithmetic from repo constants).** λ = 4.84 mm. The 3-TX loop period is 135 µs, so v_max = λ/(4·135 µs) ≈ 9.0 m/s (±). The 2-TX loop period is 90 µs, giving ≈ 13.4 m/s. A 70 m/s ball therefore aliases 4–8 times, and Doppler-FFT speed is useless without disambiguation. The repo currently gets v_r from the range track (`track.speed_ms_at`) and applies `tdm_phase = 4π·v_r·τ/λ`.
- **Sensitivity of that choice.** dφ/dv = 4π·τ/λ ≈ 0.117 rad per m/s at τ = 45 µs (6.7°/(m/s)) and ≈ 0.234 rad per m/s at τ = 90 µs (13.4°/(m/s)). For a λ/2-spaced pair, angle error ≈ δφ/(π·cosθ), which is ≈ 2.1° per 1 m/s of v_r error at 45 µs and ≈ 4.3° per 1 m/s at 90 µs. A range-rate slope with 37.5 mm bins can easily carry 0.5–1 m/s of error, and more if the track is aliased by multipath or a club return. So a few degrees of angle error, or a loss of "coherence" (see `HORIZONTAL_COHERENCE_MIN = 0.90`), is the expected result.
- **This error hits only an axis that combines phases across TX slots.** An axis measured only between simultaneously sampled RX channels has no TDM error. First check which axis (vertical LCMF vs withheld horizontal) uses inter-TX phase on the rotated LEVM mounting. If horizontal is the TDM axis, that alone plausibly explains "horizontal withheld, vertical accepted".
- **Recommended fix (high benefit, low–medium effort, low risk).** Estimate the chirp-to-chirp Doppler phase ψ of the same TX across consecutive loops, averaged over RX channels and snapshots. ψ gives v modulo λ/(2·T_loop) with mm/s precision. The true TDM phase is then ψ·(τ/T_loop) + 2πk·(τ/T_loop) for a small set of integer hypotheses k (3 for a 3-TX loop). Pick k from the coarse range-rate. The coarse speed only needs ±λ/(4·T_loop·...) accuracy, about ±3–4 m/s instead of about ±0.1 m/s, which the range track easily meets. Validate the choice with the Bechter criterion (phase continuity across an overlapping or redundant virtual element, if the LEVM layout has one) or with max array coherence over the k hypotheses. `measure_tdm_sign` already computes a loop-to-loop phase against `psi_pred` for the sign, so most of the machinery exists. This is evidence-based: Bechter measured 3.5° of error at 18 m/s and removed it.
- **Alternatively, use OPS speed.** The OPS243 already measures radial ball speed to ~0.05 m/s grid spacing. Once it is converted to the IWR line of sight with the known geometry, it can seed the TDM phase. Risk: it is a different line of sight, and the cosine correction is unvalidated.
- **Angle estimator choice matters less than coherence.** With one dominant target per range bin and N_virtual ≤ 12, conventional beamforming plus interpolation, or a 2-source (direct + image) ML fit, is appropriate. MUSIC and Capon need several snapshots with decorrelated sources. The direct path and floor image are coherent, which is exactly the case where MUSIC fails without spatial smoothing. Prefer an explicit two-ray ML fit, which is what the symmetric-ML literature does, over generic MUSIC for elevation. Compressed sensing adds little for a single ball.
- **Two-ray numbers.** Path-length difference ≈ 2·h_r·h_b/R. With radar height h_r = 51 mm (enclosure v3), ball height h_b = 0.3 m and R = 1.5 m, that is ≈ 2 cm, below the 3.75 cm range resolution even at 4 GHz. So range-domain separation (the Sensors 2020 approach) will not work at this height. Options:
  - (a) A two-ray ML fit with a floor reflection coefficient solved per session from the resting ball or a static reflector.
  - (b) Raise the radar. Separation scales with h_r; memory records that 51 mm gives ~1/6 the separation of the validated rig.
  - (c) Put RF absorber, or a non-specular textured mat, on the floor between radar and ball.
  - (d) Tilt the boresight up so the floor sits in the elevation pattern roll-off.
  Physical mitigations are cheap, but whether they work on turf is untested.
- **Calibration practice.** Far field for this array is 2D²/λ ≈ centimetres, because the aperture is only a few λ. So "5 m" is not needed for far-field reasons. The balls fly at 0.5–3 m, inside the enclosure and radome. Calibrate at 1–2 m, with the radome fitted, and across angle. A corner reflector on a turntable or rail gives a per-angle complex steering table rather than a single boresight phase vector. Radome and enclosure coupling are angle-dependent, and a boresight-only calibration cannot capture them.
- **Club tracking ("no club track").** With a 12 ms frame period (the repo fallback), a 40 m/s clubhead moves ~0.5 m per frame. `CLUB_MIN_FRAMES = 4` asks for ~36–48 ms of club presence in the beam near impact, which a clubhead may not provide. The club is also extended, its RCS changes quickly, and it aliases heavily (v_max ≈ 9–13 m/s). First check the actual frame period in v3 headers. The likely fix is to track the club on a chirp or loop time base (sub-ms) within the burst, not frame-to-frame, and to lower the frame gate. The static MTI notch also removes the club near its slow backswing and transition. This is inference to verify against dumps.

### Gaps
- I did not verify which LEVM axis uses inter-TX phase versus RX-only phase. That determination belongs in the repo, and my memory note describes the RX row as vertical on the rotated board.
- There is no public TI documentation on calibrating fast-target TDM for a 60–80 m/s target. The TDM literature is automotive (≤ ~50 m/s).
- I did not read the Häfner 2020 or arXiv 2208.03231 full texts, so no accuracy numbers are available for them.

## 3. CW Doppler (OPS243-A): speed estimation, spin from seam modulation, club/ball separation, speed calibration against references

### Takeaway
Noise is not what limits OPS speed accuracy. The limits are (1) the carrier-frequency scale (the -A is not PLL-locked and defaults to 24.125 GHz), (2) the radial-versus-total geometry (cosine), and (3) estimator bias from window, interpolation and time-varying speed. The carrier scale is checkable for about the cost of a certified K-band tuning fork. Estimator bias is removable in software with a bias-corrected 3-bin interpolator (Candan/Quinn/Jacobsen) and a ridge fit extrapolated to impact time.

### Cited Findings
- OPS24x -A sensors operate in 24.0–24.25 GHz with a default 24.125 GHz. The difference from 24.15 GHz (the K-band fork design frequency) is "an error of 1-4Hz depending on the speed". `T=n` shifts the carrier in 40 MHz steps. The OPS243-C "uses a hardware PLL … locks the frequency to within 1MHz", which implies the -A does not. — [OmniPreSense AN-026 Speed Accuracy Using Tuning Forks](https://omnipresense.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/AN-026-A_Speed-Accuracy-Using-Tuning-Fork-1.pdf)
- AN-026 procedure and results:
  - Hold a struck fork 5–10 cm above the antenna, with output set to frequency (`UH`) or speed units.
  - A 35 mph fork read 35.27/35.34 mph (within 0.9%).
  - A 2,048 Hz fork peaked at 2,056 Hz (<0.4%).
  - Detection up to a 65 mph (2,338 Hz) fork needed a lower magnitude threshold (`M>10`) and `O3`.
  - Formula: v = f·c/(2·f0). 1,024 Hz is 12.72 m/s.
  - Default resolution is 0.0607 m/s. 20 kS/s with a 4096-point FFT gives 0.0303 m/s.
  - All OPS24x designs are certified by a police radar certification lab.
  — [AN-026](https://omnipresense.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/AN-026-A_Speed-Accuracy-Using-Tuning-Fork-1.pdf)
- Police practice: the fork's narrow side must face the antenna. Two-fork tests simulate closing and receding sums and differences. Certified shops recalibrate the radar and forks once or twice a year. — [copradar.com, Speed Radar Test and Calibration](https://copradar.com/chapts/chapt3/ch3d4.html)
- FFT peak interpolation: the Jacobsen, Quinn and Candan estimators use the three DFT bins around the peak in closed form. Jacobsen and Quinn are biased, and Candan adds an N-dependent correction. Bias-corrected forms exist for arbitrary windows with or without zero padding. In comparisons, "Jain, Quinn, Improved Quinn, and Jacobsen are consistently the most effective … Improved Quinn providing a favorable trade-off". — [Comparative Analysis of Interpolation Techniques for FFT-Based Frequency Estimation (JUCS)](https://lib.jucs.org/article/156911); [Analytical solution to Jacobsen estimator for windowed signals](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/341083765_An_Analytical_Solution_to_Jacobsen_Estimator_for_Windowed_Signals); [Jacobsen, Fast Accurate Frequency Estimators](https://www.ericjacobsen.org/Files/FastFreqEstimators_SPTnT.pdf)
- Spin modulation mechanisms and processing (phase-lock tracking filter then FFT; seam at 2×; lens effect 10–25% duty): see Q1. — [US20140191896A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20140191896); [US8845442B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8845442B2/en)

### Inferences
- **Carrier-scale error.** Speed ∝ 1/f0. If the -A's actual carrier is anywhere in 24.0–24.25 GHz while the code assumes 24.125 GHz, the worst-case scale error is ±0.5%, or ±0.35 m/s (0.8 mph) at 70 m/s. A certified 24.15 GHz fork (or any known-frequency fork via `UH`) checks the frequency-to-speed chain. Because the Doppler relation is linear in f0, a low-speed fork validates the scale for all speeds. It does not validate the processing chain at ball speeds (10–13 kHz Doppler), for which the rotating target below is needed. Benefit medium, effort low (tens of dollars), risk low.
- **Resolution versus precision.** 128 samples at 30 kS/s gives a 234 Hz native bin (~1.46 m/s). Zero-padding to 4096 gives a 7.3 Hz grid (~0.045 m/s) but no new resolution. By the single-tone Cramér-Rao bound, N = 128 at 20 dB SNR gives σ_f ≈ 0.8 Hz ≈ 5 mm/s. So noise is negligible, and the error budget is window/interpolator bias, speed change within the window, and geometry. Recommendations:
  - (a) Run a bias-corrected Candan or Quinn interpolator on the un-padded 128-point FFT, or keep the padding and apply a window-specific correction.
  - (b) Use 50–75% overlapping windows for denser time samples.
  - (c) Fit the ball ridge with a low-order model (drag deceleration of order 20 m/s² plus the geometric change in radial projection) and evaluate it at the impact time. With the sound trigger's ±1.5 ms jitter, 20 m/s² contributes only ~0.03 m/s.
  Benefit small to medium, effort low, risk low.
- **Time-frequency methods.** STFT reassignment and synchrosqueezing sharpen chirping ridges, which helps club/ball separation during the ~ms overlap at impact and helps pin down the ridge onset as a radar-native impact time. These are established signal-processing tools, but I found no golf-specific evidence this pass, so treat this as theory. A simpler first step: track two ridges (club, ball) with a multi-hypothesis tracker and use the ball ridge's first sample as the impact timestamp. At 30 kS/s, a sub-ms onset is feasible and would replace the ±1.5 ms sound jitter for radar timing. TrackMan reportedly uses its radar samples for impact timing (Q1).
- **Spin on the OPS.** Current SNR is ~1.7 with confidence 0.2–0.6. Suggested order:
  - (1) Demodulate phase as well as amplitude. Remove the fitted ball ridge (instantaneous-frequency residual) and analyse the complex residual, not just |envelope|.
  - (2) Run harmonic-comb detection over candidate spin rates.
  - (3) Enforce the seam 1×/2× prior.
  - (4) Lengthen ball dwell if the buffer allows. Sample rate cannot be reduced much: 80 m/s is ≈ 12.9 kHz Doppler, and complex sampling at 30 kS/s gives ±15 kHz, so ~26 kS/s is the floor.
  Memory records that unmarked balls were "dead" and a sticker worked stationary. Radar-visible marks (a conductive stripe or foil dot) are the most plausible SNR lever on a single-channel 24 GHz sensor. Benefit is potentially high but unproven. Rules conformity of marked balls is a product question.
- **Cosine correction.** A single CW channel measures only v·cos(α). The only radar-internal way to measure α is a second line of sight, i.e. a second OPS at a known offset (Q5) or the IWR's angle. Until it is validated on the bench (rail target at known α, Q4), keep it as a candidate.

### Gaps
- OPS243-A actual carrier-frequency tolerance and temperature drift are not published in anything I read.
- I did not confirm whether the OPS243-A rolling buffer can exceed 4096 samples.
- No golf-specific evidence found for reassignment or synchrosqueezing.

## 4. Radar test fixtures and references: what produces real ground truth, and how vendors verify accuracy

### Takeaway
Tuning forks are the standard, cheap, certifiable reference for CW speed scale. Rotating reflectors are the standard constant-RCS, controllable-Doppler target for moving-target radars. Commercial RF target simulators (Mi-Wave, Eravant, dSPACE, Smartmicro, ZLY 24 GHz) are what vendors use in production and end-of-line test, but they are expensive. For a hobby bench, combine a fork (scale), a motor-driven reflector or ball (speed at ball-like Doppler and spin sidebands), a corner reflector on a turntable or rail (array phase versus angle and range bias), and gravity-defined trajectories (drop, pendulum) for angle truth.

### Cited Findings
- A certified fork is "the simplest means of validating the speed accuracy". K-band vehicle forks designed for 24.15 GHz suit the OPS24x; X- and Ka-band forks must not be used. — [AN-026](https://omnipresense.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/AN-026-A_Speed-Accuracy-Using-Tuning-Fork-1.pdf)

## 5. Would a different or additional radar configuration materially help? (IWR6843AOP/ISK, AWR1843/AWR2944, faster chirps, wider bandwidth, CW mode on the TI chip, dual OPS), with costs

### Takeaway
Most of the available gain is in processing the existing IWR6843 data (TDM phase from Doppler phase, per-angle calibration, two-ray ML) and in physical placement (radar height, floor absorber), not in new silicon. Configuration changes that help are a shorter chirp or loop period (higher v_max, smaller TDM phase), fewer TX in the burst when only one axis needs TDM, and a denser time base around impact. A second OPS for a second line of sight is the cheapest hardware addition that measures rather than assumes the cosine term. The AOP board is not an accuracy upgrade. AWR2944 is the only listed part with a plausible architectural fix (DDMA), but that is unverified here.

### Cited Findings
- DigiKey prices from a search summary, not verified by direct fetch: AWR2944EVM $786.19, IWR6843AOPEVM $195.56, AWR1843BOOST $392.44, DCA1000EVM $968.58. — [DigiKey AWR2944EVM](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/texas-instruments/AWR2944EVM/21298576); [DigiKey AWR1843BOOST](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/texas-instruments/AWR1843BOOST/10445300); [DigiKey DCA1000EVM](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/texas-instruments/DCA1000EVM/9860001); [TI IWR6843AOPEVM](https://www.ti.com/tool/IWR6843AOPEVM)
- IWR6843AOPEVM: antenna-on-package, 3 TX / 4 RX, "120° azimuth … and 120° elevation FoV". Raw ADC capture needs the DCA1000EVM over LVDS. — [TI IWR6843AOPEVM](https://www.ti.com/tool/IWR6843AOPEVM)
- TI devices ramp at up to 100 MHz/µs with idle time as low as 2 µs. v_max = λ/(4·N·T_c) for TDM. Staggering transmissions of two cascaded devices can raise v_max by K (a patent approach). — [TI SWRA553A](https://www.ti.com/lit/pdf/swra553); [US 11579282, Maximum measurable velocity in FMCW radar](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11579282)
- The OPS243-C (FMCW and Doppler) has a PLL-locked carrier (within 1 MHz), unlike the -A. — [AN-026](https://omnipresense.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/AN-026-A_Speed-Accuracy-Using-Tuning-Fork-1.pdf); [OPS243-C product page](https://omnipresense.com/product/ops243-c-fmcw-and-doppler-radar-sensor/)

### Inferences
- **Faster chirps on the current IWR6843 (high benefit, low–medium effort, medium risk).**
  - Cutting the per-chirp period from 45 µs toward ~15–20 µs means a shorter ramp, higher slope, and 2–5 µs idle. That raises v_max ~2–3× and cuts the TDM phase per m/s of speed error by the same factor (the 4.3°/(m/s) figure scales with τ).
  - The cost is fewer ADC samples per chirp, or a narrower sweep, and a shorter maximum range. Balls are within a few metres, so range is not the constraint. The ADC rate and IF bandwidth limit (0.9 × ADC rate, complex 1x) set the maximum beat frequency.
  - Risk: custom firmware changes and memory limits on burst length. Re-run array calibration after any profile change.
- **Fewer TX per loop.** If only one axis needs inter-TX phase, use 2 TX (the 90 µs loop already exists) and put the elevation or azimuth baseline on RX-only phases. Fewer TX gives a higher v_max and a smaller τ.
- **BPM-MIMO (supported in TI SDKs, from general knowledge, not verified this pass).** It combines two chirps with ±phase coding. It raises per-chirp SNR but still spans two chirp times, so fast targets still get velocity-induced errors (see the TDM/BPM disambiguation paper in Q2). Low benefit for this problem.
- **CW / continuous mode on the IWR6843.** The TI RF API has a continuous-streaming (CW) test mode, per my training knowledge, not verified in this pass. It would turn the LEVM into a coherent 60 GHz CW receiver with 4 simultaneously sampled RX: no TDM error, and phase-interferometric angles in the TrackMan/FlightScope style. At 60 GHz the Doppler is ≈ 29 kHz at 70 m/s, so the ADC rate is no issue. The unknowns are capture length and data path, which may need LVDS/DCA1000 ($969) to stream. The single TX gives up the MIMO virtual aperture, so 4 RX set the aperture. Benefit: potentially the architecture the memory note wants. Effort: high (firmware). Risk: high.
- **IWR6843AOP ($196).** Its smaller, 2-D-symmetric aperture gives worse angle precision per SNR. Its advantage is factory-consistent geometry. Not recommended as an accuracy upgrade. It could serve as a cheap second line of sight.
- **AWR1843BOOST ($392).** At 76–81 GHz (λ ≈ 3.9 mm), v_max per µs is lower and the TDM phase per m/s is higher at the same timing. No material gain.
- **AWR2944EVM ($786).** 4 TX, and (from general knowledge, unverified here) TI supports DDMA (Doppler-division MIMO) on AWR294x. DDMA transmits all TX simultaneously and removes TDM motion phase. It is the only listed part that could fix the architecture in silicon, but it is automotive-grade, higher power, and a new firmware port. Effort high, risk high.
- **Second OPS243 (dual CW).** A second radial speed from a known offset gives the in-plane velocity vector, so the cosine correction becomes a measurement. It also gives a second spin observation at a different aspect, which could help with the seam 2× and orientation dependence. OPS243-A price was not retrieved this pass. Effort low–medium (mounting, sync on the same trigger). Risk: time sync between two USB sensors.
- **OPS243-C instead of -A.** The PLL-locked carrier removes the scale uncertainty. Confirm it exposes the same raw I/Q rolling buffer before switching.
- **Wider bandwidth.** 4 GHz is already the maximum, and 3.75 cm resolution cannot separate the floor image at 51 mm radar height (Q2). More bandwidth is not available and would not suffice.
- **Placement is the cheap multiplier.** Raise the IWR, or add floor absorber or a non-specular surface between radar and tee. This addresses the two-ray problem directly. Memory already notes that the v3 enclosure's 51 mm height has ~1/6 the phase separation of the validated rig.

### Gaps
- DDMA support on AWR2944 and continuous-mode capture limits on the IWR6843 were not verified against TI documentation this pass. Treat both as leads.
- OPS243-A and OPS243-C prices and rolling-buffer parity were not retrieved.
- There is no published comparison of golf-radar accuracy versus board choice.
