# Camera subsystem improvements and camera ground truth (OpenFlight, OV9281 behind-ball, Pi 5)

Scope note: the camera's job here is club data (path, attack angle, face angle, impact location) plus the resting ball as a calibration object; radar owns ball flight. Evidence grades used below: **[D]** demonstrated/published result, **[V]** vendor or retailer claim, **[P]** patent disclosure (describes intent, not proven performance), **[I]** my inference or back-of-envelope physics, **[R]** read from the OpenFlight repo.

Key sensor facts used throughout (from the OmniVision product brief): OV9281 is 1/4", 3 µm × 3 µm global-shutter pixels, 1280×800 @120 fps, supports **2×2 monochrome binning and separately 2:1 / 4:1 subsampling**, has **FSIN (frame sync) and STROBE pins, "built-in strobe control" and "LED PWM"**, and is specified for "stable image: 0 °C to +50 °C junction temperature" — [OV9281 product brief v1.4, OmniVision](https://www.ovt.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/OV9281-PB-v1.4-WEB.pdf). Sensitivity is quoted as 13000 mV/µW·cm⁻²·s at 850 nm vs 6500 at 940 nm — [search summary of OV9281 datasheet listings](https://www.ovt.com/products/ov9281/) (datasheet itself not opened; treat the 2:1 ratio as indicative).

## 1. How do commercial camera launch monitors get club path, face angle and impact location?

### Takeaway
Every commercial camera system that reports face angle and impact location does it with **multiple viewpoints (stereo, 2–4 cameras), very short illuminated exposures (IR flash/strobe), high frame rates (240–6,000+ fps), and in most cases retro-reflective fiducial stickers on the club**; single-marker or markerless modes give speed/path/attack angle but not face angle. A monocular behind-ball camera with no markers and ambient light is outside the envelope any vendor ships for face angle, so OpenFlight should get face angle from the D-plane (ball launch direction + path) and use camera geometry for path, attack angle and impact location.

### Cited Findings
- **Foresight patent (WAWGD Inc dba Foresight Sports, priority 25 Jan 2012):** at least three, preferably four, markers on the club face at TOE TOP, TOE, TOE BOTTOM and HEEL; markers are retro-reflective with "a center with a contrasting concentric ring" ("center ... retro-reflective while the concentric ring would be matte black"); two or more calibrated stereoscopic cameras; "Lights are arranged in a radial pattern around the lenses for optimal illumination of the retro-reflective marks"; markers are labelled with geometric rules (e.g. "D1 is always longer than D2 and D3; D1 is substantially closer to horizontal"); outputs include swing path, club speed, face orientation and "the point on the club face where the club face and ball first come into contact". No frame rate, exposure or accuracy is stated. [P] — [US20130190098A1, Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20130190098A1/en)
- The same patent family describes an inertial sensor on the club to bridge times when markers are out of view, and measuring sole angle relative to the ground plane from the centre toe and heel fiducials. [P] — [Foresight patent search summary / US 8951138](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8951138)
- **GCQuad:** four cameras; frame rate is reported inconsistently — "6,000 frames per second" by retailers vs "10,000 frames per second" by GOLF.com; "photometric sensors ... regulate the flash feature on each camera", and "The slower you swing, the more light each camera must produce"; "Four removable stickers allow the cameras to measure movement, tilt, rotation". [V] — [GOLF.com, How it works: GCQuad](https://golf.com/gear/how-it-works-foresight-sports-gcquad-launch-monitor/); [Shop Indoor Golf GCQuad listing](https://shopindoorgolf.com/products/foresight-sports-gcquad-launch-monitor). Contradiction: one retailer says GCQuad club data works "without requiring reflective stickers" — [search result summary, golfersauthority.com](https://golfersauthority.com/foresight-gcquad-launch-monitor/); Foresight's own help centre documents marker application, so stickers remain the documented path — [Foresight help: club markers](https://help.foresightsports.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408197030035-How-to-Apply-and-Maintain-Club-Markers-for-Foresight-Sports-Devices).
- **GC3 vs QuadMAX:** GC3 "1-Dot Mode" (single marker high and centre on the face) gives clubhead speed, smash, attack angle and club path only; face angle and impact location need the 4-marker mode available on QuadMAX (which adds loft/lie, face angle, impact location, closure rate). [V, secondary] — [My Golf Simulator: GC3 data](https://mygolfsimulator.com/foresight-gc3-data/)
- **Uneekor EYE XO/XO2 (overhead):** two high-speed cameras and infrared sensors, cameras "can operate at over 3000 fps" [V] — [Golfstead EYE XO review](https://golfstead.com/uneekor-eye-xo-launch-monitor-review). Club data needs reflective stickers: a bar sticker and a dot sticker aligned to the horizontal centre line, bar placed "with the 7th groove from the bottom"; "Club data will be only shown if both stickers are visible" and cannot be read if the ball covers a sticker. [V] — [Uneekor EYE XO sticker reference guide (PDF)](https://hubspot.carlofet.com/hubfs/Product%20Documents/Launch%20Monitor/Uneekor/Uneekor%20EYE%20XO%20Club%20Sticker%20Guide.pdf); [Uneekor club stickers product page](https://uneekor.com/products/eye-xo-club-stickers). A newer EYE XR is marketed as "sticker-free overhead tracking" (title only; not verified) — [Top Shelf Golf EYE XR review](https://topshelfgolf.com/blogs/golf/uneekor-eye-xr-review).
- **Rapsodo MLM2PRO (behind-ball, radar + cameras):** "Impact Vision" camera at 240 fps; spin uses RPT-printed balls ("within 1%" claimed); it does not advertise face angle. [V] — [Rapsodo MLM2PRO product page](https://rapsodo.com/products/mlm2pro-mobile-launch-monitor-golf-simulator); [MyGolfSpy](https://mygolfspy.com/news-opinion/rapsodo-mlm2pro-takes-spin-measurement-to-pro-level/)
- **SkyTrak ST MAX** has no onboard swing/impact camera (ball-only photometric). [V] — [Top Shelf Golf MLM2PRO vs SkyTrak](https://topshelfgolf.com/blogs/golf/rapsodo-mlm2pro-vs-skytrak-st-max)
- **PiTrac (open-source, Raspberry Pi + Pi Global Shutter camera):** avoids expensive high-fps cameras via "infrared, strobe-based image capture"; ~500 fps detection camera and ">3,000 effective fps" strobed flight camera; strobe board and camera trigger use opto-isolation and high-voltage switching; ball detection uses OpenCV HoughCircles, called "the most disliked line of code" because of overlapping ball images and lighting sensitivity; target cost under $300. [D, hobby project] — [PiTrac on Hackaday.io](https://hackaday.io/project/195042-pitrac-the-diy-golf-launch-monitor); [PiTrac GitHub](https://github.com/PiTracLM/PiTrac)
- **Independent validation of a stereo optical LM:** Leach, Forrester, Mears & Roberts (Measurement 112:125–136, 2017) tested TrackMan Pro IIIe and Foresight GC2+HMT on 240 shots (driver, 7-iron, wedge) against a custom GOM (high-speed stereo photogrammetry) reference; "the degree of agreement ... was noticeably stronger for ball parameters, with greater variability in the clubhead parameters"; clubhead data judged adequate for coaching/fitting but to be used with caution for research. [D] — [Loughborough repository record](https://repository.lboro.ac.uk/articles/How_valid_and_accurate_are_measurements_of_golf_impact_parameters_obtained_using_commercially_available_radar_and_stereoscopic_optical_launch_monitors_/9562799); [Jon Roberts: validation of launch monitors](https://jonroberts.info/?page_id=110). Numeric limits of agreement for club parameters were behind a paywall (not obtained).
- **D-plane:** horizontal launch direction is roughly 75% face angle / 25% path for irons and ~85/15 for drivers (TrackMan/Tuxen finding, reported secondarily). [D, secondary] — [Titleist Learning Lab: club path](https://www.titleist.com/learning-lab/performance/golf-club-path); [TrackMan blog: club data definitions](https://www.trackman.com/blog/club-data-definitions)

### Inferences
- [I] **Geometry of a behind-ball camera is ill-conditioned for face angle.** Face angle is a rotation about the vertical axis; for a camera looking down the target line, a 1° yaw of a 75 mm-wide face moves heel/toe by only 75·(1−cos1°) ≈ 0.01 mm laterally and ~1.3 mm in depth; the depth change alters apparent width by ~1.3/1000 at 1 m ≈ 0.1 px. Only features with **lever arm along the line of sight** respond: a point d mm in front/behind the face moves laterally d·sin1° — ~0.4 mm for an iron (d≈25 mm), ~1.7 mm for a driver (d≈100 mm). This explains the measured ±11° silhouette spread in project notes and is why every vendor that reports face angle uses a second viewpoint or face fiducials seen obliquely.
- [I] Vendors put stickers on the **face** because their cameras are side-on (GCQuad) or overhead (Uneekor). A behind-ball camera sees the back/sole/top line, so any OpenFlight fiducials must go on surfaces visible from behind (back cavity/flange, toe, hosel, top line) and must span depth along the target line to be useful for yaw.
- [I] Practical product answer for face angle with the current hardware: **D-plane inversion** — face ≈ (launch_dir − (1−k)·path)/k with k≈0.75 (iron) / 0.85 (driver). A behind-ball camera measures horizontal launch direction and lateral club path well (both are image-plane motions). Error propagation: σ_face ≈ σ_launch/k, so a 0.5° launch-direction error gives ~0.6–0.7° face error, plus model error of the 75/85% rule (off-centre strikes/gear effect bias it). This is how TrackMan-class radar systems report face without seeing the face.
- [I] Frame rate is not the main lever for club metrics near impact; **exposure time (blur) and viewpoint** are. Vendors' high fps mostly buys multiple samples within the few centimetres before impact.

### Gaps
- No published accuracy numbers from Foresight/Uneekor patents for face angle or impact location; the GC2+HMT club-parameter limits of agreement (Leach 2017) were paywalled.
- GCQuad frame rate conflict (6,000 vs 10,000 fps) unresolved; GCQuad "no stickers needed" claim unverified.
- Bushnell Launch Pro was not researched separately (commonly described as a GC3 variant; unverified in this pass).

## 2. Illumination: pulsed NIR strobes synced to global-shutter exposure

### Takeaway
A small 850 nm pulsed LED array (4–8 high-power emitters, 20–50 µs pulses, fired from the sensor's STROBE output or a GPIO locked to frame start) plus an 850 nm band-pass filter is the single hardware change that most directly attacks the measured blur (~10 mm) and light (gain/FPN) problems; it is cheap (~$40–100) and eye-safe in strobed operation, but needs a hardware pulse-width limiter so a software fault cannot leave the LEDs on DC.

### Cited Findings
- OV9281 has STROBE and FSIN pins and "built-in strobe control"/"LED PWM". [D, vendor spec] — [OV9281 product brief](https://www.ovt.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/OV9281-PB-v1.4-WEB.pdf)
- Arducam forum: strobe output enabled by writing register **0x3006 = 1** (output mode), with 0x3928 = 0x1A or 0xFF and 0x3925–0x3927 = 0x00; meaning of each register not documented in the thread and the official strobe guide link is dead. [D, community] — [Arducam forum: OV9281 strobe registers](https://forum.arducam.com/t/how-to-set-strobes-registers-in-ov9281/906)
- InnoMaker OV9281 Pi module: supports external trigger and "LED and flash strobe mode"; trigger input is opto-isolated (TLP281); STROBE output "allows to get signal when pixel array starts integration". [V] — [InnoMaker CAM-MIPI9281RAW-V2](https://www.inno-maker.com/product/cam-mipi9281raw-v2/); [Amazon listing summary](https://www.amazon.com/Raspberry-External-Monochrome-Bullseye-libcamera/dp/B09WTP5GZH)
- External trigger on OV9281 modules: trigger pulse ≥ 2 µs, and one Arducam (UVC) variant reaches only ~100 fps in external-trigger mode because the sensor works differently from streaming mode. [V] — [Arducam wiki: OV9281 external trigger](https://docs.arducam.com/UVC-Camera/Appilcation-Note/External-Trigger-Mode/OV9281-Global-Shutter/)
- Raspberry Pi GS camera (IMX296, for comparison): XTR input at 1.8 V, exposure = low-pulse width + 14.26 µs, frame rate set by pulse rate; needs a level shifter; frame drops unless gain/AWB fixed. [D] — [raspberrypi/documentation external_trigger.adoc](https://github.com/raspberrypi/documentation/blob/master/documentation/asciidoc/accessories/camera/external_trigger.adoc)
- **LED option: ams-OSRAM SFH 4715AS (OSLON Black, 850 nm centroid / 860 nm peak, ±40° half angle, 1×1 mm chip):** max DC 1.5 A; pulsed 3 A for tp ≤ 800 µs and duty D ≤ 0.005; **1330 mW/sr and 2250 mW flux at 1.5 A, 100 µs**; rise/fall 12/15 ns at 3 A; VF 3.6 V typ at 3 A/100 µs; −0.3 %/K brightness tempco. [D, datasheet] — [SFH 4715AS datasheet v1.13 (2026-01-09)](https://look.ams-osram.com/m/3a147ddac0391319/original/SFH-4715AS.pdf)
- **IEC 62471 IR limits (as summarised by Lumileds AB191-4):** classification distance 200 mm; cornea/lens IR limit E_IR = 18000·t^−0.75 W/m² for t ≤ 1000 s and 100 W/m² beyond; risk-group emission limits: E_IR exempt 100, RG1 570, RG2 3200 W/m²; retinal thermal weak-visual-stimulus L_IR exempt ≤ 6000/α W/m²/sr (α_min 0.011 rad → 545,455 W/m²/sr); pulse duration is used as t for pulsed lamps. Worked examples: a single 940 nm LED with reflector at 1 A and a 3×3 array at 1.5 A are both **Exempt** at the stated conditions. [D, manufacturer app note] — [Lumileds AB191-4 LUXEON IR eye safety](https://lumileds.com/AB191-4-LUXEON-IR-Family-Eye-Safety-Application-Brief)
- For repetitively pulsed sources, the time-averaged exposure is compared with CW limits provided no single pulse exceeds a limit on its own. [D, secondary summary] — [Lumileds AB191-4 (search summary)](https://lumileds.com/AB191-4-LUXEON-IR-Family-Eye-Safety-Application-Brief)
- IEC 62471 exposure-time thresholds for IR eye hazard: Exempt 1000 s, RG1 100 s, RG2 10 s. [D, secondary] — [Smart Vision Lights IEC 62471 summary](https://smartvisionlights.com/wp-content/uploads/IEC_62471_summary.pdf)
- Commercial precedent: GCQuad's per-camera flash scales with swing speed [V] — [GOLF.com](https://golf.com/gear/how-it-works-foresight-sports-gcquad-launch-monitor/); Foresight patent uses ring lights around lenses to illuminate retro-reflective marks [P] — [US20130190098A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20130190098A1/en); PiTrac uses IR strobes on Pi GS cameras [D] — [PiTrac](https://hackaday.io/project/195042-pitrac-the-diy-golf-launch-monitor).
- FlightScope's camera-assisted face impact location needs ≥300 lux near the hitting area (ambient-light product, useful as a benchmark for how light-hungry un-strobed systems are). [V] — [FlightScope face impact location](https://flightscope.com/products/mevo-club-face-impact-location)

### Inferences
- [I] **Blur budget.** Blur = v·t. At 40 m/s: 300 µs → 12 mm (matches the measured ~10 mm); 75 µs → 3 mm; 30 µs → 1.2 mm; 10 µs → 0.4 mm. With a strobe inside a longer exposure window, the **pulse width, not the exposure register, sets the effective blur** — provided ambient is suppressed by an 850 nm band-pass so the ambient contributes little over the open window.
- [I] **Light budget.** 4 × SFH 4715AS at 1.5 A ≈ 5.3 W/sr on axis → ~5.3 W/m² at 1.0 m, ~15 W/m² at 0.6 m (inverse square, within the ±40° cone). Typical 300 lux white-LED room light is on the order of 1 W/m² of visible irradiance, and room LEDs emit almost nothing at 850 nm. Order-of-magnitude: a 4–8 LED strobe gives ~5–10× the current ambient signal at the club, so a 30 µs pulse can match today's 300 µs ambient exposure at similar gain — i.e. ~10× less blur at no SNR cost. This is back-of-envelope; the OV9281 absolute QE at 850 nm vs visible was not found (gap), so verify with a bench exposure test.
- [I] **Retro-reflective markers change the budget by orders of magnitude:** a ring of LEDs coaxial with the lens (Foresight-style) returns light from retro-reflective tape preferentially to the camera, so markers stay far above background even at 10–20 µs and low gain. This is the cheapest route to near-binary marker segmentation.
- [I] **Duty-cycle/thermal:** 120 fps × 50 µs = 0.6% duty (≈ the SFH 4715AS 3 A limit of D ≤ 0.005); at 450 fps × 30 µs = 1.35%. Use 1.5–2 A pulses in continuous streaming, or fire the strobe only in a **burst window** armed by the radar/sound trigger (the swing's last ~50–100 ms), which keeps average duty tiny. Check the datasheet's pulse-handling chart before exceeding 1.5 A.
- [I] **Eye safety check (strobed):** 8 LEDs at 1.5 A ≈ 10.6 W/sr → 266 W/m² at 200 mm during a pulse; time-averaged at 0.6% duty ≈ 1.6 W/m², far below the 100 W/m² exempt E_IR. A single 50 µs pulse delivers ~0.013 J/m² vs a limit (18000·t^0.25 J/m²) of ~1500 J/m². Retinal: a bare 1×1 mm chip at 1.5 A DC is ~1.33e6 W/m²/sr × R(850)≈0.5 ≈ 6.7e5 W/m²/sr, **above** the 5.45e5 exempt L_IR for long continuous viewing — so a stuck-on fault at close range could exceed exempt. Mitigation: hardware one-shot/monostable or RC-limited gate that caps pulse width (e.g. ≤100 µs) regardless of GPIO state, plus a diffuser (increases apparent source size α and lowers radiance). Get a formal IEC 62471 assessment before shipping to testers.
- [I] **850 vs 940 nm:** OV9281 is ~2× more sensitive at 850 nm (13000 vs 6500 per the listing). 850 nm shows a faint red glow; 940 nm is effectively invisible but costs ~2× the LED power. For a bench and tester rig, 850 nm is the better trade.
- [I] **Band-pass filter:** an 850 nm band-pass (or at least long-pass/IR-pass) on the M12 lens removes room light, mains-frequency LED flicker (which is visible at 75–300 µs exposures) and the need for gain >12×. Must confirm the current 2.8 mm M12 lens has no IR-cut filter (NoIR variants of these modules are sold — [BigaMart listing, "M12 NO IR Filter"](https://bigamart.com/product/raspberry-pi-industrial-camera-ov9281-external-trigger-stream-mode-monochrome-global-shutter-sensor-1mpixel-with-m12-no-ir-filter-len-fov90-for-rasp-pi-4b-3b-3b-3a-cm3-cm3-pi-zero-w/)). Cost ~$10–30.
- [I] **Sync design for Pi 5:** keep the sensor in streaming mode (full fps) and use the OV9281 STROBE output (1.8 V logic, needs level shift/opto into a low-side logic-level MOSFET driver) so the LED pulse is phase-locked to integration. If STROBE is not broken out on the module, drive from a Pi GPIO timed from frame-start timestamps — less precise (µs-level jitter with an RT-ish loop; untested). The mainline Linux ov9282 driver's support for strobe registers is unknown — likely needs raw I²C register writes (gap).
- [I] **Expected gains:** blur 10 mm → ~1 mm (toe blur of 7–10 mm is the stated limit for impact location, so this directly moves the impact-location floor toward the segmentation/pixel floor of ~1–2 mm/px); gain drops below ~4× (FPN goes away); resting ball stops saturating if the strobe/exposure is set by a ball-peak constraint; performance becomes independent of room lighting (a tester-pool problem today). Cost ~$40–100 (LEDs on star boards or a small PCB, MOSFET, gate driver, 5–12 V supply with a local storage cap, filter). Effort ~1–2 weeks including safety limiter.

### Gaps
- No readable OV9281 datasheet section on STROBE pulse width/offset registers, polarity or voltage; the Arducam thread gives only magic values.
- Absolute OV9281 QE at 850/940 nm vs 550 nm not found; the light-budget ratio above is an estimate.
- No source found for the exact IEC 62471 retinal thermal pulsed formula (L_R = 50000/(α·t^0.25)); the Lumileds table only gives simplified group limits.
- Whether the Pi OV9281 module in use exposes STROBE on a header is unconfirmed.

## 3. Calibration: intrinsics per mode, binned modes, extrinsics to radar and ground, stability, field checks

### Takeaway
Calibrate each unit once at full 1280×800 with a ChArUco board, derive the binned-mode intrinsics analytically (fx/2, cy/2 with the half-pixel convention and any crop offset), and verify each mode with a 5–10 image check; replace ball-diameter-derived focal length (circular and ±5% at ~20 px) with the board-derived value, and add a flat printed fiducial (AprilTag/ChArUco) on the mat or enclosure for a 1-second field check of intrinsics drift, camera pitch/roll and the ground plane.

### Cited Findings
- OpenCV supports calibration with ChArUco boards (detect markers → interpolate chessboard corners → calibrateCamera). [D] — [OpenCV: Calibration with ArUco and ChArUco](https://docs.opencv.org/4.13.0/da/d13/tutorial_aruco_calibration.html)
- Practitioner guidance: ~20–25 board views from different angles with corners covering the whole image; <1 px RMS reprojection error is "good", ~0.3 px typical for chessboard corners vs ~1.0 px for ArUco marker corners. [search-snippet level; practitioner sources] — [Limelight ChArUco calibration docs](https://docs.limelightvision.io/docs/docs-limelight/getting-started/performing-charuco-camera-calibration); [camcalib: what is reprojection error](https://www.camcalib.io/post/what-is-the-reprojection-error)
- OV9281 supports both **2×2 binning** and **2:1/4:1 subsampling**, plus cropping/windowing. [D] — [OV9281 product brief](https://www.ovt.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/OV9281-PB-v1.4-WEB.pdf)
- **Thermal drift:** camera warm-up raises internal temperature up to ~15 °C above ambient; resulting image drift "from tenths of pixels to several pixels"; temperature compensation reduced drift from 8.2 px to <1 px in one study; focal length and principal point vary systematically with temperature (Smith & Cope 2010, SLR). [D] — [Sensors 2022: thermal drift test stand (PMC9786690)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9786690/); [Smith & Cope, temperature effects on SLR calibration](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/268200043_THE_EFFECTS_OF_TEMPERATURE_VARIATION_ON_SINGLE-LENS-REFLEX_DIGITAL_CAMERA_CALIBRATION_PARAMETERS); [ISPRS: thermal effects on focal length](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLII-3/103/2018/isprs-archives-XLII-3-103-2018.pdf)
- OV9281 "stable image" junction range 0–50 °C. [D] — [OV9281 product brief](https://www.ovt.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/OV9281-PB-v1.4-WEB.pdf)
- **Repo today:** focal scale and camera pitch are inferred from the stationary ball's apparent diameter (`reference_ball_camera_model`, "Infer focal scale and pose from the stationary regulation-size ball"), and `focal_px = ball.diameter_px * camera_ball_range_m / ball_diameter_m`. [R] — `C:/Users/harjo/Desktop/Coding/OpenFlight/openflight-agent-review-workflow/src/openflight/camera/ball_flight.py` (lines ~150–160) and `.../camera/club_delivery.py` (line ~685)

### Inferences
- [I] **Nominal intrinsics:** fx ≈ 2.8 mm / 3 µm ≈ 933 px at 1:1 and ≈ 467 px binned — consistent with the project's measured 466.67 px (binned). Scale at range R: R/fx per pixel, i.e. ~1.07 mm/px at 1 m (1:1), ~2.1 mm/px binned. A 42.67 mm ball is ~40 px at 1 m or ~28 px at 1.4 m at 1:1 — which is why the hard-coded 9–30 px gate fails at 1:1.
- [I] **Binned/subsampled modes:** with pixel-centre convention, binning by 2 gives fx' = fx/2, fy' = fy/2, cx' = (cx − x0 + 0.5)/2 − 0.5 where x0 is the sensor crop offset of that mode (similarly cy'); normalised distortion coefficients are unchanged. The 320×200 mode is probably binning plus a crop, so its principal point shifts by the crop offset — read the mode's crop from the driver, then verify with a short board capture per mode. If a mode is **subsampling (skipping) rather than binning**, edges alias and sub-pixel edge/centroid estimates degrade; check which the driver uses (gap).
- [I] **Why ball-derived focal length is a weak calibration:** a ±1 px diameter ambiguity on a ~20 px ball is ±5% in fx and range; saturation biases diameter upward. Board calibration gets fx to well under 1% with reprojection ~0.2–0.3 px. The resting ball should become a **consistency check / range sensor using known fx**, not the source of fx.
- [I] **Lens stability:** M12 lenses are focused by a thread; any focus touch changes fx and distortion. Lock with thread-locker after focus, recalibrate after any lens change, and record a unit serial + calibration hash in session metadata.
- [I] **Field check (target <1 s, no manual input):** a printed AprilTag/ChArUco patch (≈ A5) on the hitting mat or a fixed tag on the enclosure face in view: solvePnP gives camera pitch/roll/height relative to the mat plane each session (ground plane solved per session, consistent with the "no manual setup inputs" policy), and the reprojection residual of a known tag layout flags intrinsic drift (e.g. alarm if >0.5 px). Cost ~$5; effort 1–2 days.
- [I] **Camera–radar extrinsics:** a trihedral corner reflector with an attached printed tag, placed at 2–4 positions in the hitting zone, gives matched radar range/angle and camera pose for a rigid transform fit; alternatively reuse in-situ registration from live ball tracks (the project already found +6.18°/+5.19° offsets). Standard in automotive radar–camera calibration (no source fetched in this pass).
- [I] **Thermal:** warm the Pi/camera for ~10–15 min before calibration and before bench sessions; log sensor/SoC temperature per frame; if the field-check residual trends with temperature, fit a linear fx(T), cx(T).

### Gaps
- Driver-level detail on whether 640×400 / 320×200 modes use binning or skipping and their crop offsets was not found.
- Kalibr was not researched (only relevant if an IMU/inclinometer is added).
- No quantitative thermal drift data for OV9281 + M12 plastic/glass lenses specifically.

## 4. Algorithms: resting-ball detection, clubhead tracking/6-DoF pose, face angle, sub-pixel limits, blur

### Takeaway
The resting-ball failures are mostly a **priors problem** (fixed 9–30 px gate, no ground-plane or stationarity constraint), fixable in days with geometry from calibration + radar range; clubhead face angle cannot be rescued by better segmentation on a monocular behind view, so the algorithm effort should go to path/attack angle/impact location (image-plane quantities) and D-plane face, with markers and/or a second view as the route to direct face measurement. Blur-as-signal methods (deblatting, shape-from-blur) are real but research-grade and not Pi-real-time.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- [I] **Resting-ball detector fix (highest value per effort):**
  1. Replace the four hard-coded 9–30 px gates with a gate derived from calibrated fx (per mode) × 42.67 mm / expected range, where expected range comes from the radar impact range or the enclosure geometry prior, ±25%.
  2. Add a **ground-plane prior**: the ball bottom must lie on the mat plane (from the field-check tag), so candidate centre height must equal ball radius above the plane — kills door stops and other clutter off the plane or of wrong size-for-range.
  3. Add a **stationarity prior**: the resting ball is static over N pre-trigger frames; require low temporal variance of centroid and brightness.
  4. Score candidates by radial gradient symmetry/circularity rather than blob area; fit the circle to edge points (sub-pixel), excluding saturated pixels, instead of sqrt(4A/π).
  5. Fix saturation at the source with an exposure/strobe setting constrained by the ball's peak value (project memory already calls for this).
  Expected gain: most clutter mis-picks eliminated; works at all three resolutions. Cost $0; effort 2–4 days. Evidence: physics + repo; not yet measured.
- [I] A learned detector (tiny YOLO/NanoDet at 320–640 input) is feasible on Pi 5 CPU for the **resting** ball because it only needs to run a few times per second before the shot, not per frame; but training data is the bottleneck (public sets are tiny) — use synthetic BlenderProc renders plus the project's own labelled frames. Priority below the geometric priors; use as a candidate proposer, keep geometry as the verifier.
- [I] **Clubhead tracking:** for path and attack angle, track a stable point (e.g. toe tip or a back-flange marker) across frames in the image plane and lift to 3D with range from radar and the calibrated ray model; fit one rigid motion over the last few frames (project already found a single-rotation fit is the right prior). Lateral (path) and vertical (attack) are image-plane motions and are well conditioned from behind; face angle is not (see §1).
- [I] **Face angle options, ranked:** (a) D-plane from launch direction + path — free now, ~0.6–0.8° if launch direction is ~0.5°; (b) second camera viewpoint (side or overhead) with retro-reflective markers — the vendor-proven route, likely sub-degree; (c) back-of-club markers with depth separation on a monocular view — driver-feasible (~1.7 mm/° lever arm), marginal on irons (~0.4 mm/°) unless markers extend behind the head (a bench-only clip-on fin); (d) silhouette/mesh fitting — measured ±11°, not viable.
- [I] **Sub-pixel limits:** with good contrast and SNR, centroid of a well-lit circular marker or circle fit to a strong edge typically reaches ~0.05–0.2 px; silhouette edges of a dark clubhead against clutter are far worse (the project measured ~1 px mask error). At ~1–2 mm/px this means markers (≈0.1–0.4 mm) beat silhouettes (≈1–2 mm) by roughly an order of magnitude. The sub-pixel figures are general machine-vision experience, not sourced in this pass.
- [I] **Blur:** eliminating blur optically (strobe) is far cheaper and more reliable than deconvolving it. Blur streaks do encode intra-frame velocity; a cheap use is a **consistency check** (streak length ≈ v·t_exp validates club speed against radar), not a pose estimator. Full deblatting/shape-from-blur is offline-only research on the ROCm box.
- [I] Impact location: with blur reduced to ~1 mm and markers or a crisp toe landmark, the ball-centre-in-face-coordinates method (project memory: B − r·n̂) becomes limited by pixel scale (~1–2 mm/px) rather than toe blur (7–10 mm).

### Gaps
- No published accuracy for monocular behind-ball clubhead face-angle estimation (markerless or marker-based) was found.
- Pi 5 inference latency for small detectors at 640×400 was not measured/sourced.
- The deblatting methods' runtime and robustness on elongated, specular clubheads is unknown.

## 5. Ground truth for camera club metrics on a test bench

### Takeaway
The cheapest credible ground truth is a **second, side-on (or overhead) strobed OV9281/Pi GS camera plus retro-reflective markers on a bench club**, calibrated jointly with the production camera (stereo), backed by **synthetic Blender renders with exact pose** for algorithm development, and **impact tape (not foot spray) photographed against the grooves** for impact location; research-grade references (GOM/Zeiss high-speed stereo, marker mocap, swing robots) are what papers use but are expensive.

### Cited Findings
- Leach et al. 2017 used a **GOM (high-speed stereo photogrammetry)** custom method as the gold standard; GOM uncertainty (95%) 0.06 mph ball velocity, 0.01° launch angle/direction, 14 rpm spin; clubhead agreement weaker than ball for both TrackMan and GC2+HMT. [D] — [Jon Roberts: validation of launch monitors](https://jonroberts.info/?page_id=110); [Loughborough repository](https://repository.lboro.ac.uk/articles/How_valid_and_accurate_are_measurements_of_golf_impact_parameters_obtained_using_commercially_available_radar_and_stereoscopic_optical_launch_monitors_/9562799)
- Corke et al. 2019 used markers on the clubhead and hosel with a golf robot to establish repeatability (face ≤0.2° SD); their dataset tables distinguish shots "without tape", suggesting impact tape was part of the protocol. [D] — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1754337118792798); [ResearchGate table caption](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Mean-and-standard-deviation-SD-clubhead-presentation-for-shots-without-tape-n-12-a_tbl1_326997227)
- **Synthetic data:** BlenderProc is an open-source pipeline for photorealistic procedural rendering that outputs ground truth for segmentation, depth, optical flow and **object pose** (BOP-compatible); photorealistic renders reduced the sim-to-real gap vs. less realistic domain randomisation for segmentation/normal estimation. [D] — [BlenderProc (Semantic Scholar)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/BlenderProc:-Reducing-the-Reality-Gap-with-Denninger-Sundermeyer/87d2a16af38839f4970d5e75882ce293a3b45854); applied to texture-less industrial parts [D] — [arXiv 2402.04979](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.04979v1)
- **Impact marks:** impact tape leaves a dark mark where the ball compressed and is easy to photograph; foot spray is cheap but "gives you a blurry mark rather than a precise one" and can affect ball flight (vendor blog selling an alternative spray — biased). [V] — [InkPact blog](https://inkpact.golf/blogs/news/the-best-golf-impact-tape-alternatives-and-why-spray-wins-every-time); tape/spray as standard practice [V] — [HackMotion](https://hackmotion.com/impact-position-checkpoints/)
- Grooves are used by Uneekor as the sticker alignment reference ("7th groove from the bottom"), i.e. grooves are a practical face coordinate system. [V] — [Uneekor sticker guide](https://hubspot.carlofet.com/hubfs/Product%20Documents/Launch%20Monitor/Uneekor/Uneekor%20EYE%20XO%20Club%20Sticker%20Guide.pdf)
- Commercial camera LMs (GCQuad/QuadMAX) report impact location and are used as fitting references; TrackMan 4 reports impact location via radar+camera (OERT). [V] — [Popular Science on QuadMAX development](https://www.popsci.com/gear/foresight-sports-quadmax-photometric-golf-launch-monitor-development/); [TrackMan 4](https://www.trackman.com/golf/launch-monitors/trackman-4)

### Inferences
- [I] **Tiered ground-truth plan (cost / what it validates / expected truth accuracy):**
  1. **Synthetic (Blender/BlenderProc, Cycles with motion blur set to the real exposure):** $0, 1–2 weeks. Render the official-dimension head (Mizuno/Srixon per project policy) with the calibrated intrinsics, the enclosure pose, 850 nm-like lighting and blur at 30/75/300 µs. Exact pose/face angle/impact point truth; ideal for measuring algorithm error vs blur and resolution and for training detectors.   2. **Second reference camera (stereo truth):** second OV9281 or Pi GS camera side-on 1–2 m from the ball, strobed, with 3–4 retro-reflective dots (on toe, heel, top line/back) — the Foresight arrangement. Pi 5 has two camera connectors (well-known; not re-verified here), so both can run on one Pi with a shared strobe for sync. Expected truth: face/path to well under 1° if markers are ~0.2 px and baseline is wide (estimate). Cost ~$60–120. Effort 2–3 weeks incl. stereo calibration with the ChArUco board.
  3. **Marker bench club for the production camera:** retro-reflective dots on surfaces visible from behind, with at least one pair separated along the target line (a clip-on bench fin for irons). Gives production-camera face angle truth directly. $10–20.
  4. **Impact location truth:** impact tape (crisp mark) photographed square-on with a printed groove-aligned mm grid or with grooves as the scale; mark centroid by ellipse fit; expected ~1 mm repeatability (estimate). Keep foot spray for quick visual checks only (blurry edges). Pressure-sensitive film (e.g. Fujifilm Prescale) could give contact-area shape but was not researched (gap).
  5. **Borrowed commercial reference:** simultaneous capture with a GCQuad/QuadMAX or TM4 at a fitter (comparator set per project policy: TM4 / Full Swing KIT / Mevo Gen 2); cheap per session but commercial club data is itself less reliable than ball data (Leach 2017).
  6. **Expensive, flag only:** GOM/Zeiss high-speed stereo DIC, optical mocap (Vicon/Qualisys with high-fps cameras), swing robots (Golf Laboratories-class) — thousands to tens of thousands of dollars; access via a university sports-engineering lab (Loughborough, Ulster) rather than purchase.
