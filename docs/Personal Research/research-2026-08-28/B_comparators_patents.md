## 1. TrackMan 4 — OERT

### 1.1 The patent family, verified by direct fetch

**CONFIRMED.** [US 10,953,303 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10953303B2/en) ("System and method for determining impact characteristics of sports ball striking element"), inventor Fredrik Tuxen (TrackMan's CTO), **assignee Trackman A/S**. This is the root of the family **[PRIOR** identified the chain 10,953,303 → 11,439,886 → 11,612,801 → **12,263,393**]; I fetched the terminal member directly: [US 12,263,393 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12263393B2/en) confirms **assignee Trackman AS**, priority date 2017‑12‑22, filed 2023‑02‑28, granted 2025‑04‑01 — a straight continuation chain, same assignee throughout, now also broadened to bats and rackets.

**What the camera contributes — verbatim from Claim 1 of the root patent (CONFIRMED, direct quote):**
> "identifying a position of a first point on an object striking element in a first image and a second image from a sequence of images received from an imager, wherein the first and second images are captured within a predetermined time span of an impact... determining an impact time of the impact... determining a position in at least one of the first and second images corresponding to a location of the object at the impact time... determining a distance from the imager to a location at which the object and the object striking element impact one another; and determining an impact location of the object relative to the first point."

So the claimed method is: **track a fix-point on the club across a short window of frames bracketing impact, use the radar-set impact instant to pick out the frame(s) that matter, and read the ball-contact point relative to that fix-point.** The patent's own text also states the imager "may operate with a known frame rate of typically **150 to 4000 fps**" — this is markedly higher than the **60 fps** TM4 actually ships (§1.2), which means TM4's shipping product sits at the *low end* of what this patent family claims, or a different, unpublished refinement handles the gap between 60 fps frames (consistent with **[PRIOR]**'s "impact location falls out of the fusion, not out of frame rate" reading).

**On a stored geometric model — reconciling a discrepancy.** The specification text does contain the "known geometric properties" language **[PRIOR]** flagged: *"The memory may further store geometric models or reference images of specific golf clubs or golf club types... comparing the position of the first point with the known geometric properties to determine a three-dimensional location."* This directly supports [PRIOR]'s reading that the shipping product likely blends fix-points with a stored club-shape prior. A separate automated pass over the same patent, asked to quote fix-point detection language, characterized it as using "motion detection, edge detection, and neural networks — not geometric model matching"; that description is **not corroborated by a verbatim quote** in this pass and looks like a stretch/summarization artifact rather than a distinct patent passage — **treat the "known geometric properties" sentence above as the load-bearing verbatim text, and disregard the neural-network characterization as unverified.**

### 1.2 What TM4 actually ships, and the accuracy TrackMan publishes

**CONFIRMED, direct fetch of [trackman.com/golf/launch-monitors/tech-specs](https://www.trackman.com/golf/launch-monitors/tech-specs):** TM4's camera is **"HD 720p @ 60 fps"** / "Full HD 1080p @ 45 fps," described only as having "High Dynamic Range," with **no illumination spec listed on this page** (700–800 lux continuous LED spot is **[PRIOR]**, sourced from TrackMan's indoor-setup support docs, not this spec sheet). **TrackMan publishes no numeric accuracy/tolerance figure anywhere on its technical-specifications page for face angle, dynamic loft, attack angle, club path, or impact location** — the page lists these as tracked parameters with no ± value. This is a **negative finding worth weighing against the marketing language**: TrackMan's blog calls OERT's impact-location output "**exact**" and describes ["4D silhouette clubhead tracking"](https://www.trackman.com/blog/golf/two-radars-one-camera-zero-doubt) **[PRIOR cites this same blog]**, but no first-party page states a number.

**CONFIRMED, [trackman.com/blog/club-data-definitions](https://www.trackman.com/blog/club-data-definitions):**
> "Trackman directly measures the 3D silhouette of the club head and uses this to determine the geometric center of the club head" — with the stated intent of requiring **no assumptions of club-head design**.
> "For Face Angle and Dynamic Loft, Trackman uses the orientation of the club face at the center point of where the ball is in contact with the club face" — i.e., readings are taken *at the impact point*, not at the geometric face center, and TrackMan states face angle/dynamic loft "can change 1–2 degrees during the impact interval" between address and full compression.
> A **worked sensitivity number, CONFIRMED**: "a 10 mm impact towards the toe will cause the Face Angle to be 2 degrees more open at the impact location when compared to the center of the club face" for a standard driver — i.e. **face-angle sensitivity to impact-location error ≈ 0.2°/mm**, TrackMan's own number, independent of and consistent with **[PRIOR]**'s gear-effect citation ("10 mm toe strike ≈ 2° face shift").

**Is impact location markerless on TM4?** **[PRIOR]** already establishes yes — OERT's own marketing states "Impact Location Without Markers." This pass adds nothing new here beyond confirming the mechanism is patent-documented (§1.1), not just a marketing claim.

**Independent (non-TrackMan) accuracy validation found:** only for the **older TrackMan Pro IIIe**, not TM4/OERT — **[PRIOR]** already cites Leach et al. 2017 (>65% of face-angle/dynamic-loft readings within ±1° of a traceable optical reference, club parameters less accurate than ball parameters). A 2024 peer-reviewed TM4 study exists (["Trackman 4: within- and between-session reliability..."](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02640414.2024.2314864)) but — per its own abstract framing — establishes **repeatability**, not absolute accuracy against an external ground truth; I did not find a peer-reviewed absolute-accuracy study of TM4's OERT-derived face angle, dynamic loft, or impact-location output specifically. **UNKNOWN / not published** is the honest state of TM4 OERT club-data accuracy outside TrackMan's own unquantified "exact" language.

## 2. FlightScope Mevo Gen 2 / Mevo+ — precisely which metrics need a sticker

This is the ground-rules item with the most actionable new material. **[PRIOR]** already established the two-tier product structure (base unit = radar-only kinematics + spin; Pro Package ≈ $1,000 add-on = D-plane club-delivery metrics; Face Impact Location ≈ $499 add-on = camera metric) and that FlightScope states Pro-Package face angle "is not measured directly but calculated from measured path and H launch data" **[PRIOR, §1F(a)]**. This pass pins down the sticker question metric-by-metric with direct-fetch quotes.

### 2.1 Spin — sticker requirement is unconditional indoors, absent outdoors

**CONFIRMED, [FlightScope FAQ](https://flightscope.com/pages/faq):** *"Aluminum stickers are needed in an indoor environment in order for the X3C, Mevo+, Mevo Gen2, and Mevo to accurately measure ball spin."* A companion product page ([flightscope.com/products/flightscope-aluminum-stickers](https://flightscope.com/products/flightscope-aluminum-stickers)) sells the sticker specifically for this, with placement instructions (sticker must face the target, away from the club face). **Outdoors, no sticker is required** for spin (ambient light + longer flight arc give the radar/camera enough signal) — matching **[PRIOR]**'s "markerless outdoors" note on Mevo Gen 2 spin.

### 2.2 Club path / face-to-path / face-to-target / dynamic loft (Pro Package) — no sticker, but not camera either

**[PRIOR, confirmed, §1F(a)]** already has the load-bearing FlightScope quote that Pro-Package face angle is *calculated*, not measured, from path + launch data — i.e. this is a **pure D-plane inversion off radar-measured ball launch and club path**, independent of any camera image and independent of any ball marking. This pass finds no FlightScope source contradicting that; the FAQ and product pages never mention stickers in connection with Pro Package metrics.

### 2.3 Face Impact Location — camera-based, light-gated, and the sticker question is genuinely ambiguous in FlightScope's own material

**CONFIRMED, [FlightScope FAQ](https://flightscope.com/pages/faq), direct quote:** *"FlightScope's Patented Advanced Fusion Tracking requires a minimum of 300 lux to capture accurate Face Impact Location data. For the best results, we recommend a consistent reading above 300 lux within a 3-foot radius of the hitting location."* This matches **[PRIOR]**'s figure exactly and is now sourced to the FAQ page directly rather than only a press summary.

**On stickers specifically for this feature, FlightScope's own pages do not give one consistent, first-party answer.** A search-engine synthesis (not independently verified against a single page) reported: *"Indoors (Limited Flight): Aluminum stickers are required for limited flight indoor use with Face Impact Location. Outdoors: There are no stickers or ball requirements for outdoor use."* Direct fetch of the [Face Impact Location product page](https://flightscope.com/products/mevo-club-face-impact-location) did **not** corroborate this with an explicit vendor sentence — the page discusses only the 300 lux figure and "Advanced Fusion Tracking" by name — and instead surfaced two **customer reviews** (not vendor claims) that disagree with each other: one says *"No longer need to put a sticker on my club head to look at face impact"* (implying an **earlier** generation of the feature needed a sticker **on the clubhead**, not the ball), another says *"Works good once you have it lined up and use RCT balls."*

**Verdict on this specific sub-question: [CONFIRMED] Face Impact Location is camera/Fusion-Tracking-based and light-gated at 300 lux, independent of the Pro Package's radar D-plane path. [UNKNOWN, conflicting] whether a ball or club marker is required for it specifically** — FlightScope's own pages do not settle it, and the only direct testimony is contradictory user anecdote. The one clean, source-backed statement is the **spin** requirement (§2.1); do not conflate "Mevo needs a sticker for spin" with "Mevo needs a sticker for face angle/impact location" — the ground-rules framing that treats these as one requirement is not what FlightScope's own material supports.

### 2.4 FlightScope/EDH patents — what they actually claim, verified

**[PRIOR]** names three FlightScope/EDH patents (US 10,338,209 "Fusion Tracking," US 10,151,831 spin-axis, plus a general reference to "camera+radar fusion"). Direct fetch adds verbatim confirmation and one important negative finding:

- **[US 10,338,209 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10338209B2/en)**, assignee **EDH US LLC**, inventor Henri Johnson. **CONFIRMED, abstract quoted verbatim:** *"calibrating a perspective of an image of a camera to a perspective of a Doppler radar for simultaneous tracking of the moving sports object... combining a camera measurement of an angular position of the moving sports object with a simultaneous Doppler measurement of a radial distance, speed or other measurement."* The fusion method described is a **Kalman filter** ("Radar and camera tracking data can be combined in an optimal manner... implemented by a tracking filter such as a Kalman filter or similar"). **This patent's scope is exclusively ball/object position tracking — it never claims clubhead orientation or face angle.** This directly corroborates **[PRIOR]**'s "no camera-based clubhead pose (radar carries club geometry)" verdict for FlightScope, now with the actual claim language.
- **[US 10,151,831 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10151831/en)**, assignee **EDH US LLC**, inventor Henri Johnson — the ≥3-receiver spin-axis patent **[PRIOR]** cites. **CONFIRMED, new finding:** this patent explicitly **argues against** camera-based measurement: *"High speed cameras are costly and suffer from difficulties in alignment, illumination, and measurement reliability."* It computes spin axis purely from **inter-receiver timing**: `PHI = arctan[SH·TH/(SV·TV)]`, needing "at least three receivers spaced in a predetermined manner" (four for full horizontal+vertical resolution). **No camera appears anywhere in this patent.** This is a clean, direct piece of evidence that FlightScope's *radar-only* spin-axis path and its *camera-assisted* Face Impact Location path are architecturally and IP-wise separate lineages, not one fused system — consistent with, and sharpening, **[PRIOR]**'s "radar carries spin, camera carries impact location" split.
- A newer FlightScope-family patent, [US 12,544,624](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12544624/en) ("Determining spin based on club parameters"), infers **spin from trajectory + inferred club identity** (matching observed launch angle/ball speed/club speed/carry against per-club spin-probability tables) — a third, statistical/lookup-based route to spin that is neither camera nor direct radar-Doppler measurement. Not previously in the evidence base; **CONFIRMED to exist, mechanism read from a search-engine abstract summary, not yet a verbatim-quote fetch** — flag as a secondary source pending direct verification if this patent becomes load-bearing.

## 3. Full Swing KIT — radar-only for face angle, camera is video-only

**[PRIOR]** already has the headline finding, sourced to [US 11,311,789 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11311789B2/en): face angle is **"Calculated (Club and ball path off tee)"** from **CW/FMCW radar** signals, with the camera doing swing video only. Direct re-fetch of the patent in this pass **confirms and sharpens** this:

**CONFIRMED, assignee Full Swing Golf Inc, inventors John DeLeon / Daniel Nicora / Zongbo Wang.** Direct quote: *"Current golf launch monitors use radars and/or cameras"* — a candid acknowledgment in the patent's own background section that camera-based competitors exist — followed by a specification that **never uses the word "camera" again outside that one background sentence**. Every club/ball parameter in the patent, face angle included, is derived from the dual CW+FMCW radar returns. **There is no camera-based fallback or fusion path described anywhere in this patent for any club metric.**

**What the built-in 4K camera is for:** every product-side source (Full Swing's own [KIT tech page](https://www.fullswinggolf.com/kit-launch-monitor-technology/), retailer listings) describes it purely as **swing video capture / slow-motion playback**, exported at 1080p to the app — never as a data source. No Full Swing patent found in this pass (or **[PRIOR]**) describes deriving any club metric optically. The commonly repeated marketing phrase "machine-learning enhanced radar" and third-party claims of "combines radar data with image processing for accuracy" (**[UNSOURCED — a retailer-review characterization, not corroborated by the patent or by Full Swing's own technical page**, which describes ML applied to the radar returns themselves, not fused with imagery]) should be read skeptically against the patent's plain text. **Net: Full Swing KIT is the one comparator of the three with a patent-documented, unambiguous radar-only mechanism for face angle — no camera role of any kind in that computation.** [PRIOR]'s "16 outputs, no face-impact-location metric" also stands unchallenged; no source in this pass found Full Swing publishing an impact-location output at all.

**Independent accuracy:** [Carl's Place indoor/outdoor accuracy test](https://www.carlofet.com/blog/full-swing-kit-accuracy-test-indoors-vs-outdoors) and various reviews report **~2–3% distance agreement with reference measurement outdoors**, and one MyGolfSpy comparison reportedly found deviations from a GCQuad up to ~50% on some metrics (reported via a search summary, not independently re-verified against the original MyGolfSpy article in this pass — **UNKNOWN/unverified magnitude**, but the direction — Full Swing's own numbers being reasonably solid on ball-flight distance and considerably more contested on some club-side metric — is consistent with a radar-only, model-driven face-angle computation that has no independent optical cross-check.

That conclusion is not re-derived here.

What is new in this pass, specific to **golf clubhead/impact measurement** (as opposed to generic object pose):

- **Rapsodo's own patent, read directly — [US 9,171,211 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9171211B2/en), assignee Rapsodo Pte Ltd** [EXCLUDED — Rapsodo/MLM2PRO is not a comparator, cited only because it is the closest DIY-relevant camera-vision golf patent]. **CONFIRMED by direct fetch:** this patent describes a **two-camera stereo** system with the object placed a short, fixed distance in front of it — explicitly **not** modeled on a behind-ball single-camera vantage, and it addresses only "speed, elevation angle, azimuth angle, spin rate, and spin axis" — **no face angle or impact location claim at all**. This narrows what Rapsodo's IP actually covers relative to **[PRIOR]**'s broader "US 11,077,351 / US 11,583,746 — per-club CAD scan + stereo silhouette registration (<1° RMS)" note — that RMS figure lives in the *later* Rapsodo family (11,077,351), not this earlier one, and I could not independently re-verify the <1° RMS number by direct quote in this pass (the USPTO PDF for 11,077,351 is CCITT-fax-encoded image data and resisted text extraction twice) — **treat the <1° RMS figure as [PRIOR]-sourced, not re-confirmed here.**
- **A 2023 markerless, YOLO-based camera system for clubface impact position, found via search but not pinned to a citable source in this pass.** Multiple independent search queries surfaced a consistent description — a "real-time golf swing measurement system... high-speed vision and a customized YOLO-based segmentation model... directly measures the impact position and quantifies the deviation from the sweet spot along both horizontal and vertical axes... eliminating the need for embedded sensors or radar," validated on beginner vs. professional golfers — associated with a researcher named **Jaeyeop Choi**, but I was unable to resolve this to a specific DOI, journal, or fetchable full text despite five distinct search attempts. **This is flagged, not used as evidence.** If it exists as described, it would be the first academic (non-patent) demonstration of markerless camera-only impact-location detection — but per the ground rules, an unverifiable claim gets no weight. **UNKNOWN — needs a follow-up literature search with journal-database access (e.g., Google Scholar, IEEE Xplore direct) rather than general web search**, which kept surfacing search-engine paraphrases instead of the source.
- **A related, verified 2023 paper is a different system: [Location Matters — Can a Smart Golf Club Detect Where the Club Face Hits the Ball?, Sensors 23(24):9783](https://doi.org/10.3390/s23249783).** **CONFIRMED to exist and be about impact-location sensing**, but it is an **embedded-sensor "smart club" system, not a camera/vision system** — relevant only as a boundary marker (it shows the impact-location problem attracts non-optical solutions too), not as camera-vantage evidence. Do not cite this for a vision claim.
- **No paper was found, in this pass or [PRIOR], using a golf-club CAD model plus a monocular shaft-line/hosel-tangent fit specifically from a down-the-line or behind-the-ball camera and reporting a face-angle or impact-location accuracy number.** The closest documented artifact remains the **Rapsodo patent's stereo hosel–ferrule line + CAD registration claim** [PRIOR], which is stereo, not monocular, and whose accuracy number I could not re-confirm by direct quote in this pass (previous bullet). The academic literature search in this pass corroborates, rather than overturns, **[PRIOR]**'s conclusion that this is unexplored territory in the peer-reviewed record — OpenFlight's own `openflight_club_pose_research_survey.md` measurement (silhouette IoU 0.633, orientation unusable) is still the most concrete data point that exists anywhere for a small (20–40 px), behind-ball, single-camera clubhead.

## 6. What this settles for OpenFlight's question

Restating only the load-bearing conclusion, not repeating the "why it matters" framing already in the task brief: **of the three comparators, only Trackman 4 optically measures anything about the clubhead itself (a fix-point tracked across a handful of 60 fps frames, radar-timed to impact) — and even that patent-documented mechanism never claims to read the face plane's orientation directly; face angle and dynamic loft are read off the fitted club-orientation model at the contact point, gear-effect-corrected by the camera-derived impact location, not measured as a face-normal from pixels.** FlightScope's Pro-Package face/loft is pure D-plane inversion off radar (no camera, no vision problem to solve at all). FlightScope's Face Impact Location is genuinely camera-based but is a **position-on-a-known-shape** problem (where did the ball touch a rigid known face plane), not an **orientation-of-an-unmarked-object** problem — a materially easier task than what a behind-ball silhouette would need to solve for face angle. Full Swing's face angle is radar-only, full stop, by its own patent's plain text. **No comparator reads clubface orientation from an image the way a behind-ball silhouette fit would need to.** This corroborates, with new primary-source detail, the conclusion `openflight_club_pose_research_survey.md` and the v2 guide already reached independently: the commercial answer to "how do you get face angle from behind the ball" is "you don't look at the face — you infer it from where the ball went, or you don't look from behind at all."
